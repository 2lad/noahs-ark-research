Module 02 • Historical Progression

Chronological History of the Ark Tradition & Expeditions

From Early Bronze Age cuneiform tablets in Mesopotamia to high-frequency ground-penetrating radar arrays on the slopes of Mount Tendürek and Mount Ararat.

c. 2150 – 1700 BCE Mesopotamian Cuneiform Records

Sumerian Eridu Genesis & Akkadian Atra-Hasis

The earliest surviving flood accounts—the Sumerian Eridu Genesis (hero Ziusudra) and the Old Babylonian Atra-Hasis Epic—describe a divinely warned patriarch constructing a massive bitumen-caulked vessel to preserve human and animal life from a catastrophic deluge in southern Mesopotamia (likely linked to the ~2900 BCE Shuruppak riverine flood stratum).

c. 1200 – 650 BCE Neo-Assyrian & Hebrew Canon

Epic of Gilgamesh (Tablet XI) & Genesis 6–9

Tablet XI of the Epic of Gilgamesh (recovered from Ashurbanipal's library at Nineveh) recounts Utnapishtim's cubic vessel landing on Mount Nimush (Pir Omar Gudrun in Iraqi Kurdistan). Concurrently, the Hebrew Book of Genesis records Noah constructing a rectangular 300 × 50 × 30 cubit cypress/gopher-wood vessel landing upon "the mountains of Ararat" (the ancient Kingdom of Urartu in Eastern Anatolia).

290 BCE – 94 CE Classical Historiography

Berossus of Babylon & Flavius Josephus

Babylonian priest Berossus (3rd century BCE) and Romano-Jewish historian Flavius Josephus (Antiquities of the Jews, Book I, 94 CE) report that local inhabitants in Armenia/Cordyene still visited the remains of the vessel, scraping bitumen pitch from its timbers to use as protective amulets.

1829 – 1955 CE High-Altitude Ararat Ascents

Parrot's First Ascent & Navarra's Hand-Tooled Timber

Friedrich Parrot completes the first recorded ascent of Greater Mount Ararat (5,137 m) in 1829. In 1955, French explorer Fernand Navarra retrieves a 1.5-meter hand-squared oak beam from beneath the Ahora Gorge ice cap. Subsequent radiocarbon (C-14) dating by multiple laboratories placed Navarra's wood in the 7th–8th century CE (Byzantine/Early Islamic era), rather than the Bronze Age.

SEPTEMBER 1959 Aerial Photogrammetry Discovery

Captain İlhan Durupınar's Stereo-Mapping Discovery

While examining NATO/Turkish Geodetic Survey aerial photographs of the Doğubayazıt region, Turkish Army Captain İlhan Durupınar identifies a symmetrical, 157-meter boat-shaped contour embedded in a mudflow 29 km south of Greater Mount Ararat. A 1960 joint expedition (including George Vandeman and Arthur Brandenberger) dynamites a small section, finding soil and rock rather than an intact wooden hull, leading mainstream geologists to classify it as a doubly plunging syncline.

1977 – 1987 CE Early Subsurface Surveys

Ron Wyatt, David Fasold & Turkish Government Dedication

Following a 1978 earthquake that sheared away surrounding mudstone, Ron Wyatt and marine salvage expert David Fasold conduct early subsurface interface radar (SIR) and metal-detector sweeps, reporting iron-oxide concentrations and orthogonal grid patterns. In 1987, the Turkish government formally designates the site as the Nuh'un Gemisi (Noah's Ark) National Park.

2019 – NOVEMBER 2026 Current Multi-University Research

Noah’s Ark Scans, Sivas Cumhuriyet University & Nov 2026 Timber Core

Between 2019 and 2025, 3D Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR) and Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT) reveal sharp right-angle subsurface structures and three distinct internal horizons. In June 2026, Turkish authorities grant an official excavation permit led by Prof. Dr. Cenker Atila (Sivas Cumhuriyet University). In November 2026, researchers announce the recovery of charred wood fragments ~9 meters below the southern slope showing apparent tool marks, now undergoing radiocarbon and botanical analysis across four universities.

Syncline Fold vs. Buried Structure Hypothesis

Geologists (e.g., Lorence Collins) interpret Durupınar as an eroded doubly plunging syncline in a basaltic/limonitic mudflow, whereas geophysicists cite sharp 90° subsurface GPR reflections as anomalous for natural folding.

Regional Quaternary Stratigraphy

Major Holocene Flood Horizons in the Near East

While mainstream geology rejects a single global Pleistocene/Holocene inundation covering 5,000-meter peaks simultaneously, multiple major regional flood events are firmly documented in the archaeological record:

  • 1. The ~2900 BCE Mesopotamian Alluvial Floods (Shuruppak, Ur & Kish): Excavations by Leonard Woolley and Max Mallowan uncovered sterile water-laid silt deposits up to 3.4 meters thick separating Ubaid and Early Dynastic strata, matching the exact era of the Sumerian king Ziusudra.
  • 2. The Black Sea Deluge Hypothesis (~5600 BCE / ~7,600 BP): Proposed by Columbia University geologists William Ryan and Walter Pitman: Mediterranean seawater breached the Bosporus sill, raising Black Sea water levels by ~100 meters and inundating 100,000 km² of inhabited coastal shelf just north of Anatolia.
  • 3. Durupınar Marine Clay & Micro-Fauna (3,500–5,000 BP): 2021–2023 core samples analyzed by Istanbul Technical University (İTÜ) and Ağrı İbrahim Çeçen University identified clayey marine materials and single-celled marine organisms within the site matrix dating to the Chalcolithic/Early Bronze Age transition.

The Arzap / Kazan Drogue (Anchor) Stones

13 MONOLITHS

Approximately 15–20 km west-northwest of the Durupınar site near the villages of Kazan (Arzap) and Yaygınyurt stand over a dozen massive basalt and andesite megaliths (up to 3 meters tall, weighing 4–10 metric tons), each carved with a beveled, biconical rope-hole near the apex.

Maritime Hypothesis: Heavy stone drogue anchors suspended from a vessel's hull to stabilize pitch/roll in storm swells, cut loose before grounding.
Archaeological Counter-View: Urartian cultic stelae or medieval Armenian khachkar memorial stones (note: several bear Byzantine-era crosses carved into their faces).

Petrified Wood & Metal Assay Claims

LABORATORY ASSAYS

Prior to the November 2026 deep-core timber extraction, earlier expeditions tested surface specimens from Durupınar with mixed analytical outcomes:

  • 1991 Galbraith Lab Thin-Section Analysis: A specimen touted as "petrified laminated deck timber" was examined via petrographic microscopy and identified as natural travertine/limestone banded by iron-manganese oxide dendrites rather than cellular wood anatomy.
  • Iron Oxide / Alloy Readings: Subsurface metal-detector surveys detected regular grid-like ferromagnetic anomalies (70–90% iron oxide). Proponents interpret these as oxidized iron rivets/fittings; geologists attribute them to natural magnetite/limonite concentrations in weathered volcanic basalt.

NOVEMBER 2026 EXCAVATION REPORT Permit: Sivas Cumhuriyet University

Charred Wood Fragments Recovered at 9 Meters Depth Beneath Southern Slope

Following formal excavation authorization granted by Turkish authorities in June 2026, an international research team led by Prof. Dr. Cenker Atila (Department of Archaeology, Sivas Cumhuriyet University) in collaboration with the Noah's Ark Scans geophysical consortium excavated targeted test trenches along the southern flank of the Durupınar formation.

At a depth of approximately 9 meters (29.5 feet) beneath compacted mudflow strata—directly correlating with the lower structural horizon identified in earlier 3D Ground-Penetrating Radar (GPR) tomograms—excavators recovered dark, partially carbonized wood fragments. Preliminary macroscopic inspection indicates linear striations consistent with ancient hand-tool shaping, alongside traces of mineralized dark resin/pitch.

Current Laboratory Status (As of Nov 2026): Samples have been sealed under chain-of-custody protocols and distributed to four independent university laboratories—including Bar-Ilan University in Israel and partner institutions in Türkiye and Europe—for Accelerator Mass Spectrometry (AMS) radiocarbon dating, dendrological species identification, and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) tool-mark verification.

PHASE I • 2019–2023

3D GPR & Electrical Resistivity (ERT)

Multi-frequency Ground-Penetrating Radar and Electrical Resistivity Tomography transects mapped the interior of the 157m formation down to 20 meters, revealing:

  • ■ Sharp 90° Right-Angle Geometries: High-contrast dielectric boundaries intersecting at orthogonal angles, contrasting with natural parabolic geological folds.
  • ■ Three Horizontal Deck Planes: Distinct reflective layers at ~3m, ~6m, and ~9m depths corresponding to a three-story interior layout.
  • ■ Central Corridor & Void Spaces: Elongated low-resistivity cavities flanked by parallel longitudinal wall signatures.
METHOD: 100MHz & 250MHz Dual-Array GPR
PHASE II • 2021–2024

Soil Core Geochemistry & Marine Micro-Fauna

Nearly 30 soil and rock core samples extracted from inside and immediately outside the perimeter were analyzed by İTÜ and Ağrı İbrahim Çeçen University:

  • ■ Marine Clay & Micro-Organisms: Presence of clayey marine sediments and unicellular marine organisms at ~2,000 meters above sea level.
  • ■ Chalcolithic / EBA Date Window: Sediment dating clustered between 3,500 and 5,000 years BP (3000–1500 BCE), aligning with ancient flood chronology.
  • ■ Elevated Organic Carbon & Potassium: Significantly higher organic matter and potassium inside the formation boundary than in surrounding volcanic soil.
LABS: Istanbul Technical Univ. (İTÜ) & AİÇÜ

Methodological Caveats & Open Questions

Objective archaeological standards require holding conclusions provisional until independent peer-reviewed replication is published:

  • ▲ Pending C-14 Verification: Until the four universities publish AMS radiocarbon dates on the Nov 2026 wood, contamination by younger root systems or medieval campfires/structures cannot be ruled out.
